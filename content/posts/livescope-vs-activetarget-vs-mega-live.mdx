Forward-facing sonar — often called live sonar — is the biggest change to fish finding in a decade. Instead of showing you a scrolling history of what you've already driven over, it shows you fish and structure **in front of the boat, in real time**, moving as they move. You can watch a fish approach your lure and react.

Three systems dominate: **Garmin LiveScope**, **Lowrance ActiveTarget**, and **Humminbird MEGA Live**. They all do the same core job. The differences that actually matter to a buyer are narrower than the marketing suggests — and the biggest factor in your decision probably isn't image quality at all.

Here's the honest version.

## The single most important thing to understand first

**The transducer is not the whole purchase.**

Every one of these systems is a *transducer plus black box* that must connect to a **compatible chartplotter/display from the same brand**. There is no cross-compatibility. You cannot run LiveScope on a Humminbird head unit, or MEGA Live on a Lowrance.

So the real question is rarely "which live sonar is sharpest?" It's:

> *What display do I already own, and what will the complete working system cost me?*

A realistic full setup includes:

- **The live sonar system itself** — roughly $1,300–1,600
- **A compatible display**, if you don't already have one — commonly $700–1,500 for a screen large enough to be worth it
- **A dedicated 12V power source** — most anglers add a separate lithium battery, since these units draw meaningfully more than a traditional sonar
- **A mount** — usually a trolling motor barrel mount or a dedicated live sonar pole so you can rotate the transducer independently

It is completely normal for a first-time live sonar setup to land between **$2,500 and $4,000** all-in. If a deal looks like it's only $1,300, check carefully whether a display is included.

**This is why brand loyalty dominates this category.** If you already run a modern Garmin, Lowrance or Humminbird unit, staying in that ecosystem usually saves you four figures — and that saving outweighs the modest image-quality differences between the three.

## Garmin LiveScope Plus (LVS34)

Garmin got here first — LiveScope launched in 2018, several years ahead of the competition — and that head start still shows in ecosystem maturity, community knowledge, and the sheer number of anglers who can help you tune it.

**Strengths**

- Widely regarded as the benchmark for target separation — distinguishing two fish close together, or a fish sitting tight to cover
- Particularly strong in **shallow water**, where cone angle and noise rejection get difficult
- Largest install base, which means the most tutorials, settings guides and secondhand availability
- Beam coverage of roughly 20° x 135°, giving a tall usable window in forward mode

**Trade-offs**

- Requires a compatible Garmin display (the ECHOMAP UHD2 series and above are the common pairing)
- Premium pricing, and Garmin discounts less aggressively than competitors

**Best for:** anglers already in the Garmin ecosystem, and anyone who fishes shallow water or heavy cover where target separation matters most.

## Lowrance ActiveTarget 2

ActiveTarget 2 is Lowrance's second-generation live sonar, and it closed most of the gap that existed with the first version.

**Strengths**

- Highest stated resolution of the three on paper
- Same 200 ft range class as LiveScope
- Natural choice if you run Lowrance HDS — Lowrance's down-scan imaging is excellent, so a Lowrance boat gets a genuinely strong all-round package
- Lowrance runs promotional pricing more often than Garmin, so real-world cost can come in lower

**Trade-offs**

- Several anglers report LiveScope still edges it in very shallow water and in scout mode
- Requires a compatible Lowrance display

**Best for:** existing Lowrance owners, and buyers who want flagship-class live sonar but are price-sensitive and willing to watch for promotions.

## Humminbird MEGA Live 2

MEGA Live 2 is Humminbird's answer, and it's a real improvement on the original MEGA Live.

**Strengths**

- **Wider cone** than LiveScope — fish enter the picture earlier and stay visible longer, which some anglers strongly prefer for tracking movement
- Image quality generally placed between Garmin's older LVS32 and current LVS34
- Pairs with Humminbird's outstanding MEGA Side Imaging, which is arguably the best side imaging available

**Trade-offs**

- **Shorter maximum range — around 150 ft, versus roughly 200 ft for the other two.** For most freshwater fishing this is irrelevant; for deep-water work it may not be
- **Compatibility is much narrower than people assume — see the warning below**

> **Check your display before you buy.** MEGA Live 2 works only with **XPLORE, APEX and SOLIX G3** units. It is **not compatible with HELIX**, and not compatible with older **SOLIX G1 or G2** units.
>
> This is the most expensive mistake available in this category. If you run a HELIX — one of the most popular fish finders Humminbird has ever sold — MEGA Live 2 will not work with it, and you would need to replace your display as well. Budget accordingly, or look at the original MEGA Live instead.

**Best for:** anglers already running an XPLORE, APEX or SOLIX G3, who value a wider field of view and rely on MEGA Side Imaging.

## So which one should you actually buy?

Be honest about which situation you're in:

**You already own a modern Garmin, Lowrance or Humminbird display.**
Buy that brand's live sonar — but **confirm your exact model is supported first**, because "same brand" does not guarantee compatibility. Humminbird HELIX owners in particular cannot run MEGA Live 2. Where your display is supported, the image-quality differences between these three are small enough that they don't justify replacing a working unit, and staying put typically saves $700–1,500.

**You're starting from nothing and money is no object.**
Garmin LiveScope Plus. It has the deepest ecosystem, the strongest shallow-water reputation, and the best resale value.

**You're starting from nothing and value matters.**
Compare complete bundle prices — display plus live sonar together — from all three brands before deciding. Lowrance and Humminbird bundles frequently undercut Garmin, and the gap in on-water performance is smaller than the gap in price.

**You mostly fish deep water.**
Favour LiveScope or ActiveTarget for the longer usable range.

**You mostly fish shallow, or heavy cover.**
LiveScope's target separation reputation is well earned here.

## Is live sonar worth it at all?

For most casual anglers, honestly: **no.**

If you fish a handful of weekends a year, $2,500–4,000 buys an enormous amount of other fishing. A good $400–700 CHIRP and down-imaging combo will find fish perfectly well.

Live sonar makes clear sense if you're:

- Fishing competitively, where locating and targeting individual fish is the whole game
- On the water very frequently
- Targeting suspended fish — crappie, and open-water bass especially — where watching a lure relative to a fish changes your catch rate dramatically

It's a genuinely transformative tool for the anglers it suits, and an expensive novelty for everyone else. There's no shame in the latter — just go in with clear eyes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I run LiveScope on a Lowrance or Humminbird unit?**
No. Each live sonar system only works with compatible displays from the same brand. There is no cross-brand compatibility.

**Do I need a separate battery?**
Most anglers add one. Live sonar draws considerably more power than traditional sonar, and running it off your main starting battery risks leaving you stranded. A dedicated lithium battery is the standard solution.

**Can I use live sonar from a kayak?**
Yes, and it's increasingly common — but it requires careful planning around mounting, battery capacity and total weight. Expect the setup to cost more than the kayak for many anglers. Our [kayak fish finder guide](/best-kayak-fish-finder/) covers more practical options.

**Will these work through ice?**
All three brands offer ice-fishing configurations or bundles, though specifics vary by model and season. Confirm the exact bundle contents before buying — see our [ice fishing fish finder guide](/best-ice-fishing-fish-finder/) for dedicated ice options.

**Is the older LiveScope LVS32 still worth buying used?**
It can be good value. The LVS34 improves target separation and shallow-water performance, but an LVS32 at the right price still massively outperforms having no live sonar at all.
